## May 2026 — trade carries, credit drags

**Retail sales fell 0.6% year on year**, the first outright contraction of the
year and a step down from the low-single-digit growth that held through the
spring. The goods trade-in subsidies that flattered late-2025 durable-goods
sales have clearly stopped adding to the headline — and with consumer inflation
back at +1.2%, real volumes are running slightly weaker than even that nominal
print suggests.

**Prices have turned, but this is reflation from below.** Factory-gate PPI,
after a long spell in deflation, has swung sharply positive — +3.9% in May,
having crossed zero only in March — and now runs well ahead of CPI, which is
stuck near +1%. The gap is worth watching: if firms cannot pass factory-gate
increases on to consumers, margins get squeezed rather than restored.

**Trade is the one bright corner; credit is no help.** Exports grew 19.4% in
US-dollar terms and the manufacturing PMI is hovering right at the 50 line.
Imports surged 27% — striking, but read it with commodity prices and last
year's low base in mind before calling it a domestic-demand revival, because
the credit data point the other way: cumulative new bank lending is running
about 13% below a year earlier and still deteriorating, April's new social
financing all but dried up (with the bank-loan component outright negative),
and M1 growth keeps trailing M2 — money parked, not deployed. With fixed asset
investment still deep in contraction on the property drag, the burden of
holding up growth keeps falling on external demand.
